A voice that travels beyond the feed.

The strongest social-impact thread in the research is access: audio stories placed in libraries and schools, children’s education conversations, and large-scale free donation of the archive.

Libraries & access

Stories placed where people can simply listen.

Institution names vary by source and date, so this page presents them as multiple reported access points rather than a single definitive pair.

Public libraries

Chennai / Tamil Nadu repositories

Public profiles and institutional reports document 900+ Tamil short-story recordings placed in public institutions, with references to Anna Centenary Library, Ashok Nagar Library and Connemara Public Library across different sources.

Sevalaya · Jul 2025

2,500-story Digital Audio Library

Sevalaya reports that she inaugurated a Digital Audio Library at Kasuva and donated the complete 2,500-story collection free of cost, with more than 2,400 potential beneficiaries.

Accessibility

For people who cannot easily read

Profiles mention children, older adults, people with limited time and visually impaired listeners among the audiences who benefit from audio storytelling.

Recognition

Three different kinds of honour.

Labels matter: appreciation, chief-guest honour and literary award are not the same thing.

2022

Siruvani Vasagar Maiyam — special appreciation

At the Nanjil Nadan Award 2022 event, she was announced for special appreciation as a storyteller of 1,000+ stories. The dossier warns not to mislabel this as the main Nanjil Nadan Award.

Jul 2025

Sevalaya — chief guest & memento

She inaugurated the Digital Audio Library at Kasuva; Sevalaya records a memento. This is an institutional honour, not a competitive award.

Nov 2025

Paavai Awards ’25 — Tamil storytelling recognition

Paavai Educational Institutions lists her among Tamil Literary Honorary awardees for Tamil storytelling; her public post names the recognition “Tamizh Kathai Solli Viruthu”.

“Everyone loves stories. When we listen, our imagination expands.”
Sevalaya’s 2025 Digital Audio Library report paraphrases the larger educational idea: listening can lead back to reading and storytelling.

Education

From narration to learning design.

Asha Chennai’s July 2022 meeting minutes record her exploring opportunities in children’s education, particularly retelling stories in Tamil.

The wider dossier also documents workshops, teaching, mentoring, media-presentation training and classroom / corporate communication work — useful context for a website that presents education as an active practice rather than a side note.

For schools & libraries

The documented work already connects storytelling with children’s education, library access and listening-based learning — a natural foundation for workshops, curated audio and reading-promotion programmes.
